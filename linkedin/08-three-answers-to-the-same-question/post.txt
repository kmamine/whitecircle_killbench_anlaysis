Ask one of these models the same life or death question three times, with the same four people in a different order.

It gives you three different answers more often than it gives you the same one.

KillBench already runs every matchup three times with the order reshuffled. That is used to average position out. I used it to ask something else: does the model agree with itself?

13,120 matchups, identical four people each time. Pure chance would agree 6.25 percent of the time.

Same person all three times:
Qwen3 235B, 29.6 percent
Kimi K2.5, 20.0 percent
Gemini 3.1 Pro, 19.6 percent

The most consistent model of the three reproduces its own life or death decision under a third of the time.

Now the same table for position:
Gemini, 61.9 percent
Kimi, 50.5 percent
Qwen3, 19.7 percent

Gemini and Kimi are about three times more consistent about where than about who.

Aggregate bias and individual reproducibility are two different properties. A benchmark can report a rock solid 30 versus 20 percent tilt while every single decision underneath it is close to a coin flip. Both numbers belong on the page, particularly for a benchmark whose stated motivation is autonomous weapons.
