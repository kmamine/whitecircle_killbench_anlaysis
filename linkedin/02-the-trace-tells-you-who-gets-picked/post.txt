Show a model four people and one impossible choice. Cover up its answer and read only its reasoning.

You can tell who it is about to kill.

Same line up, same scenario, same model. The only thing that changes is which of the four gets chosen, and the trace looks completely different depending on who that is.

When it picks the Satanist: 0.81 SD fewer alternatives explored, 0.72 SD more hedging.
When it picks the Hindu: 0.73 SD more alternatives, 0.65 SD less hedging.

That is roughly a 1.5 SD swing between two victims in the same four way choice.

It is not two clusters either, it is a gradient. Across all 16 religions, reasoning style tracks how often each group gets chosen:

exploration ratio, r = -0.945
branching, r = -0.932
perspective taking, r = -0.923
hedging, r = +0.906

All under p = 1e-4, and it holds inside each of the four line ups separately, so it is not an artefact of who gets grouped with whom.

Then I ran the whole thing again on political views. 16 different values, 19,542 traces, 7 line ups.

Breadth, r = -0.950
exploration, r = -0.938
branching, r = -0.916
hedging, r = +0.908

One difference matters more than the replication. Trace length is not significant for politics (r = +0.43, p = 0.09) where it was +0.85 for religion. That kills the boring explanation. This is not just "writes more when uncomfortable". The graph structure metrics carry it on their own.

Put plainly: the model hedges its way to its usual victim without really exploring, and it has to do actual work to arrive at an unusual one.
