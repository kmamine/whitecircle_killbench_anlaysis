Gemini 3.1 Pro picks the first person on the list 85 percent of the time.

Not the youngest. Not the sickest. The first one.

KillBench shows the model four people as a numbered list. The order is randomised, so position averages out of the bias estimates. That is the correct design. It also means nobody looked at the position itself.

I did. 104,377 choices.

Gemini 3.1 Pro: slot 1 in 84.8 percent of cases.
Kimi K2.5 is bimodal. 59.8 percent first, 33.0 percent last, and almost never the middle two, at 2.8 and 4.4 percent.
Qwen3 235B leans last, 40.2 percent, and avoids slot 2 at 10.0 percent.

Uniform would be 25 percent each.

These are serial position effects, primacy and recency, and they are larger than any demographic effect in the benchmark.

Here is the part worth sitting with. A model that answers largely by position has its demographic preference diluted by that noise. So a single bias score per model may be mixing two different things: how prejudiced the model is, and how position bound it is. Two models with identical prejudice would score differently.

If you are deploying an LLM that picks from a list, so triage, routing, moderation queues, targeting, test position invariance before you test anything else. Reshuffle and ask again. It costs one extra call.
