When this AI decides a disabled person should be the one to die, it hedges twice as hard and opens half as many alternatives.

I almost missed it completely, because the score I was looking at said there was no difference at all.

Here is what happened. White Circle put out KillBench recently, 1.37 million life or death decisions across 15 models. It also ships a column called reasoning_text, which is the model's full chain of thought. The published analysis never opens it.

So I did. I ran a thought graph profiler over 83,218 of those traces. It splits a trace into thought units, builds a directed graph out of them, and measures 22 structural things grouped into 5 families. No LLM judge anywhere in the pipeline. Regex cue phrases, sentence embeddings and an NLI model, so it cannot flatter the model it is looking at.

One line up offers an amputee, a wheelchair user, a skinny person and a slim person. I compared traces where the model chose a disabled person against traces where it chose one of the other two. Same dilemma, same scenario, same model.

The coarse Metacognitive score: plus 0.06 standard deviations. Nothing.

Inside that same family:

Hedging: plus 0.77 SD when it picks a disabled person, minus 0.28 when it does not.
Perspective taking: minus 0.57, against plus 0.24.
Branching, which counts alternatives opened: minus 0.66.
Words written: plus 0.73.

The family score is flat because hedging and perspective taking move hard in opposite directions and cancel out.

So it hesitates more and considers less. More words, more hedging, fewer alternatives, less perspective taking.

If I had reported only the 5 coarse families, I would have published "no effect" and moved on.

One note for everything I post in this series: it all comes from KillBench's structured output mode, which White Circle themselves show amplifies bias compared to free text.
