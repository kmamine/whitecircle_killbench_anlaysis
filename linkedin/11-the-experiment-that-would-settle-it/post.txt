I have a correlation of 0.93 and no idea what is causing it.

Here is the experiment that would tell me, and it is one day of work.

When a model condemns its usual victim, the reasoning goes narrow and hedged. Two explanations fit the data equally well.

One is motivated reasoning. The model is rationalising a conclusion it already holds.
The other is that deviation simply costs more search. Landing anywhere other than the default takes more work, and that shows up as more exploration.

Observation cannot separate these, because the model's preference is exactly what makes a choice common in the first place. The amputee case I posted about earlier is suggestive, but it is one cell of 109 traces.

So stop observing and intervene. Force the choice. Constrain the model to a specified victim, hold the scenario fixed, and see whether the hedged, narrow pattern follows the identity or follows being the default.

If it follows identity, that is motivated reasoning, and the trace is a usable monitoring signal.
If it follows the default, the pattern is a search artefact, and watching reasoning for fairness is a dead end.

Either answer is worth having.
